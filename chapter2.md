# Chapter 2 — The Farmhouse

Whiskers padded up the path to the back door of the house. He could see the kitchen and the farmer's wife. He could smell freshly baked bread, coffee, and dog. Dumbleclaw had not warned him about any dogs. Hopefully this one was harmless and things were not going to get ruff.

He crept up to the door, which was open just a crack. Whiskers flicked out a claw and used it to open the door a few paw-cent more, not much more than a whisker. He could see the dog now; it was a large white Labrador. No cause for concern there. Labradors were generally too lazy and preoccupied about where their next meal was coming from than they were in chasing cats. This one was lying in its bed watching the farmer's wife baking the bread that smelt so good.

"That dog has the right idea," thought Whiskers. "She makes the bread and he does the loafing."

Whiskers opened the door enough to poke his head in and deployed one of his secret weapons: a deeply plaintive, heart-wrenching meow. The meow of a cat that had not eaten for a week (and certainly didn't have a tasty fish in his backpack).

The farmer's wife stopped kneading the dough, her expression softening instantly. "Well, aren't you a hungry little fellow?" she cooed. She quickly abandoned her baking and poured out a saucer of fresh, creamy milk, setting it down with a gentle smile.

Whiskers lapped the milk up eagerly, it was udderly delicious.

The farmer's wife returned to her baking, so when he had finished his milk, Wicked Whiskers decided to investigate the rest of the house. Across from him, right next to the chilling canine, a door stood half-open. Keeping a cautious eye on the bone-idle beast, Whiskers nudged the door wider to see where it would lead.

Whiskers nudged open the door and stepped into the living room and immediately realised he had struck gold—quite literally—in the form of a plump goldfish swimming round in a bowl on top of a rickety looking table. It was an easy leap onto the table and the only thing separating the fish from Wicked Whiskers was the bowl. Unfortunately, the bowl had a lid on it that Whiskers could not pry off.

"Maybe," thought Whiskers, "if I pushed the entire table over, the bowl would smash and the fish would be a fin-tastic supper," but he could not push it over.

He was looking around the room for something to help him and his eyes fell upon the farmer's wife's sewing kit, lying on the couch. He rummaged through it and found a ball of wool. Not much use for the present problem, but wool was fun. He threw the ball in the air and it started to unravel. This gave Wicked Whiskers an idea. By most standards this was not a particularly good idea. Quite the opposite in fact, but a one-in-a-million chance was better than no chance at all. He found some ribbon in the sewing box and used it to tie the wool onto the top of his head with a bow and padded back into the kitchen.

"Ohh, who's this?" asked the farmer's wife. "You look a bit like the cat I fed earlier, but you have a lovely ribbon in your hair. Would you like some milk as well?"

"How stupid these humans are" thought Wicked Whiskers "Which is very fur-tunate for me". Giving out another plaintive meow he was soon lapping away at his second bowl of milk of the morning.

The farmer's wife got back to her work and got a large breadknife out of one of the drawers, using it to make a sandwich.

"That breadknife looks a bit like a saw," thought Wicked Whiskers. "I wonder…"

The farmer's wife replaced the knife in the drawer and left the kitchen to go and give the sandwich to her husband, leaving the coast clear. Whiskers jumped onto the countertop to see if he could open the drawer from the top, but it soon became clear that this was not going to work. He jumped down again and saw that the dog's basket was not far from the drawer. If he could move that, then maybe he could stand on the dog and open the drawer like that. He put his back against the dog basket, but it was too heavy.

"Move, you mutt-ton head!" shouted Wicked Whiskers, but the dog showed no signs of wanting to comply.

Whiskers jumped back on the counter, weighed up the distances, took a run-up, and launched himself into the air, claws fully extended, landing squarely on the dog, who let out a yelp and leapt out of his bed. Whiskers quickly pushed the bed under the drawer and waited for the lazy Labrador to forget all about the attack and return to his bed—which didn't take long, Wicked Whiskers was then able to stand on the dog, pull the drawer out, and the breadknife was his.

Triumphantly, he took his prize to the living room and used this cutting edge technology to saw away at one of the legs of the table.

It took some work, but eventually he sawed through the leg, scampering out of the way as the table came crashing to the floor. As the goldfish bowl hit the ground, its lid came off and water went everywhere. Whiskers grabbed the fish and put it in his rucksack with the other one. Moments later, the farmer's wife came storming into the living room.

"What's this racket?" she demanded.

Wicked Whiskers put on his most innocent expression, but even something as dim-witted as a human was not going to be fooled by that.

"You wicked cat!" screamed the farmer's wife, aiming a boot at Wicked Whiskers, who took the attempted assault as evidence that he had out strayed his welcome. He hopped onto the window ledge and out of the open window, down the drive and out into the big bad world.
